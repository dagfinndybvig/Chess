# Chess rules — short recap

Chess is played on an 8x8 board of 64 squares. Two players take turns:
White moves first, then Black. Each side starts with 16 pieces — king,
queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns.

- **Piece moves** — the knight jumps in an L-shape and is the only piece
  that can leap over others. The bishop slides on diagonals, the rook
  on ranks and files, and the queen on all lines, any distance until
  blocked. The king moves one square in any direction. Pawns move one
  square forward (two from their start rank) and capture diagonally
  forward.
- **Check** — when a king is attacked, it is in check; the player must
  respond by moving the king, blocking the attack, or capturing the
  attacker.
- **Checkmate** — a check the player cannot escape. The game ends;
  checkmate is the goal.
- **Stalemate** — the player to move is not in check but has no legal
  move. The game is a draw.
- **Castling** — once per game, the king may move two squares toward a
  rook and the rook jumps to the king's far side, if neither piece has
  moved, the path is empty, and the king does not pass through or land
  on an attacked square.
- **En passant** — a pawn that advances two squares may be captured by
  an enemy pawn as if it had moved only one square, but only
  immediately on the next move.
- **Promotion** — a pawn reaching the far rank becomes a queen, rook,
  bishop, or knight (this game always promotes to queen — the common
  case).
- **Draws** — the fifty-move rule (no capture or pawn move for 100
  half-moves), threefold repetition of the same position, insufficient
  mating material, stalemate, or agreement.

That is essentially the whole game — the depth is in the strategy, not
the rules.

## Learning more

- [FIDE Laws of Chess](https://handbook.fide.org/chapter/E012023) —
  the official rules.
- [Lichess](https://lichess.org/) — free play and training, no account
  needed to browse.
- [Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/) — lessons and puzzles for every
  level.
